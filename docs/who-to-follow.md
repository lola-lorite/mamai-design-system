## 1. Marie-Claire Dean — start here

**<https://marieclairedean.substack.com>** · *Delegative UX: designing the agent
experience*, 24 Sep 2026

The best single thing written on this. Her definition:

> "Delegative UX is what lets a person see, track and understand work being done in
> their name, by an agent."

Her concrete guidance, which reads like a design brief you could work from tomorrow:

- Show what the agent is doing **now**, and where.
- Show how far it got, what's still open, what it's waiting on.
- Explain **why** it chose what it chose — and what it assumed, and what it decided
  against.
- Be generous with visibility at first: show "the work, the reasoning and the
  near-misses." Then reduce it as trust builds, task by task.
- Design for the people **affected** by an agent's actions who didn't delegate it.
- Put consequential actions behind a gate. Label each one reversible or not; send
  the uncertain ones for human approval.
- Give the agent somewhere to reach people other than a chat thread — an inbox
  with state.

Read that list, then read `AGENTS.md` in this repo. It's the same discipline at a
smaller scale. This is the field you said you wanted to move into, written as
something you can do.

## 3. Pritam Roy — the distinction that clears up the jargon

**<https://pritamr.substack.com>** · *Agent-First Web*

Useful mainly for one clarification you'll need when people throw acronyms around:

- **AX** (Agent Experience) = how an *agent* handles your site. Machine-facing.
- **AXD** (Agentic Experience Design) = a *person's* experience of supervising an
  agent. Human-facing.

AXD is your field. AX is a technical concern. People conflate them constantly;
you'll sound sharp for knowing the difference.
