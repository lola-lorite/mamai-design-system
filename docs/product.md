# Context: my situation, my struggles and the MamAI project

## Who I am
- Lola, product and visual designer with ~10 years of experience: branding, product design, information design, illustration, animation, design systems.
- MSc in Information Design, BA in Graphic Arts and Painting. Fine-art background. I think visually and love beautiful UI that tells a story.
- From Málaga, living in the Netherlands for ~11 years. I speak Spanish, English and Dutch.
- New mum. My days start differently every day and my time comes in small, unpredictable blocks.

## What I'm struggling with
**Fear about my role**
- Product design is changing fast. Figma may fade, and design systems are moving into code (GitHub, Claude Code).
- What I love most (crafting beautiful screens and effects) feels like it's becoming less valued, because AI can produce it fast.
- I lost an assignment to a "design engineer": someone who designs *and* builds in code with AI. His site is plain, but that's what companies want now.
- I'm afraid that what makes me unique won't be relevant anymore.

**Overwhelm and noise**
- LinkedIn, Instagram and colleagues all say "use this skill / loop / plugin." I don't know where to start.
- I want to learn everything at once (GitHub, Claude Code, agents, token efficiency, AI tools) and end up learning nothing.
- I feel below average while everyone else seems organised.
- Short-form content is addictive and mostly noise. Few creators are actually useful to me (e.g. yeho.ux on agent experience design).
- Resources go out of date fast, which makes learning them feel pointless.

**Time and energy**
- Sleep deprivation. Hard to keep a routine when the baby wakes at 5 one day and I sleep in the next.
- I'm juggling being a mother, a product designer in the AI era, and raising a multilingual child (I want to teach him Spanish).

## What I've realised so far
- My gap is a **skills gap, not a talent gap**: I need to be able to steer the tools myself. Skills gaps close.
- My eye still matters: taste, structuring information and storytelling. AI makes many options, and someone has to know which one is right.
- Learn the **ideas behind tools** (versions, clear instructions, design tokens), not the tools themselves. Those last.
- **One small project beats a hundred saved reels.**
- The direction of UX: from designing screens to designing how AI behaves (agent experience design / AXD).

## The project: MamAI (working title, also "Ay, MamAI")
Becoming a mum during the boom of AI as a product designer. The name nods to Rigoberta Bandini's song "Ay mamá."

Homepage line (my words): *Our jobs are changing fast, it's hard to keep up, and we're exhausted (hello, sleep deprivation).*

**User:** me. Others in the same spot are a bonus.

### Lean UX Canvas (in progress)
1. **Problem:** I can't follow a routine. My days change (the baby wakes at 5, or my husband takes the morning and I sleep longer) and then I lose track of when to brush my teeth, shower or have a nice cup of tea; when to play with my son and which games fit his development; how to teach him Spanish; and when to find time to understand product design in the AI world.
2. **Outcomes:** a structured routine that adapts to how the day goes. Better planning, a way to follow both his development and mine as a designer, time and space to learn without being overwhelmed. Clarity.
3. **Users:** me.
4. **User benefits:** clarity, planning.
